# Evidence Base and Research Queue

Reviewed 2026-10-27. The initial 149-item catalog is a **working map synthesized from common social creative practices**, not 149 individually validated empirical findings. The following sources ground the structure, platform surfaces, and psychological framing; examples and performance evidence still need to be collected for individual entries.

  - Distinguishes creative structure, hooks, trends, production grammar, attention and sound. Advertising guidance is not direct causal evidence of organic performance.
- TikTok creative tips: https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/tiktok-creative-tips-finder/pc/en
- TikTok creative advertising guide: https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/guides/what-is-ad-creative-guide

- YouTube community posts: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7124474
  - Text, images, GIFs, polls, quizzes and shared videos.

## Peer-reviewed psychological evidence
- Berger & Milkman (2012), What Makes Online Content Viral? https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.10.0353
  - Observational evidence that emotion and arousal are associated with sharing. Does not imply negative arousal should be artificially induced.
- Loewenstein (1994), The Psychology of Curiosity: https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75
  - Information-gap account of curiosity. Not a universally validated hook formula.
- Pan et al. (2025), Influencer Marketing Effectiveness: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-024-01052-7
  - Meta-analysis of 251 papers; follower, post, and creator factors connect differently to marketing outcomes.
- Han et al. (2024), Meta-analysis of Social Media Influencer Impact: https://doi.org/10.1002/mar.21927
  - Credibility, expertise, trust, congruence and relationships are relevant to outcomes.
- Systematic review of influencer parasocial relationships (2026): https://doi.org/10.1108/JCM-02-2024-6611
  - Relationship mechanisms exist but much research examines nontransactional outcomes.
- Uses and gratifications social media engagement (2024): https://doi.org/10.1080/23311975.2024.2389252
  - Entertainment, identity, social contact, information, benefit, and empowerment are useful categories.
- Uses and gratifications meta-analysis (2024): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38663155/
  - Also flags problematic use risks, especially process and self-presentation gratifications.
- Parasocial engagement mental health systematic review (2026): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42623515/
  - Relationship-building can have both beneficial and problematic associations.
