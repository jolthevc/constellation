# Audience, Content and Property Psychology

Last reviewed: 2026-10-27
Status: evidence-informed strategic framework, not a validated causal model.

## The three levels

### 1. Audience psychology: why this audience uses media
Understand:
- **Context:** platform, time of day, habitual moments, other social activities, attention available.
- **Jobs sought:** entertainment, relief, social connection, information, self-expression, identity, aspiration, competence, agency, sensory reward, or practical utility.
- **Self-concept:** who audiences believe they are, aspire to become, and wish to signal. Avoid assuming these from demographics.
- **Community:** belonging, shared values, acceptable humor, social norms, in-group language, cultural references.
- **Tensions:** what problem, curiosity, opportunity, or emotional state makes content feel personally relevant. Do not exploit vulnerabilities.
- **Trust calibration:** what constitutes evidence, credibility, authenticity, and acceptable sponsor behavior for this group.
- **Decision context:** actual product usage occasions, ability to pay, alternatives, friction, and ethical commercial fit.
- **Variation:** subgroups, cultural and geographical differences, dissenting audience behavior, confidence, missing evidence.

The purpose is to discover *why*, not to stereotyping a demographic as one psychology.

### 2. Content psychology: why this specific piece performs
Model the entire viewer journey:
1. **Notice:** What visual, verbal, social, or contextual cue breaks through attention? Is it relevant rather than merely loud?
2. **Commit:** What clear promise or open question justifies spending time? Can the payoff realistically be delivered?
3. **Continue:** What escalating information, story progression, pattern, emotional attachment, or utility sustains attention?
4. **Reward:** Does the work fulfill its promise, help, move, entertain, or satisfy the viewer?
5. **Express:** Why would someone save, comment, share, remix, or discuss it? What does that action mean socially?
6. **Return:** What recurring value or identity makes future content worth seeking?
7. **Act commercially:** Is the offer relevant, credible, clearly disclosed, and congruent with the relationship?

Short-term engagement is not identical to audience satisfaction. Watch time, rewatch, useful saves, following, downstream retention and sentiment can conflict. Use multiple outcomes.

### 3. Property psychology: why someone builds a lasting relationship with this property
A property must make a repeatable and differentiated **psychological promise**:
- **Value promise:** the recurring reward someone expects.
- **Emotional signature:** the feeling after engaging, not just the subject covered.
- **Identity role:** what following this property says about the follower, if anything.
- **Relational stance:** mentor, witty friend, curious reporter, trusted specialist, mischievous character, or impartial institution. No pretending synthetic characters are real people.
- **Credibility contract:** domain expertise, transparent sourcing, good judgment, consistency, corrections.
- **Worldview and taste:** distinctive judgment and creative point of view; why this property exists in a crowded feed.
- **Habit:** recognizable series, cadence, repeatable questions, and earned anticipation.
- **Trust boundaries:** things we will not claim, fake, endorse, exploit, or sell.
- **Commercial permission:** where recommendations naturally serve the audience rather than contaminate the relationship.

## Psychological mechanisms and evidence
The machine-readable mechanism vocabulary is `mechanisms.yaml`. It includes psychological, creative and descriptive labels. A tag is a **testable heuristic**, not proof that a particular cue has a universal effect.

Sources:
- Uses and gratifications research distinguishes information, entertainment, identity, social connection and other motivations. https://doi.org/10.1080/23311975.2024.2389252
- Curiosity can emerge from perceived information gaps. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75
- Emotional arousal correlates with transmission in one influential study of news sharing; do not overgeneralize to all feeds. https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.10.0353
- Influencer effectiveness depends on follower, post and creator characteristics; transactional effects differ from nontransactional effects. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-024-01052-7
- Trust, expertise, similarity, congruence and parasocial relationships have relevant but heterogeneous evidence. https://doi.org/10.1002/mar.21927 and https://doi.org/10.1108/JCM-02-2024-6611
- Parasocial engagement can have both beneficial and problematic correlations. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42623515/

## Research and testing standard

For each claim, record one of:
- **Observed:** directly visible in public posts, comments or platform analytics.
- **Supported:** plausible inference supported by a specified external study, with applicability caveats.
- **Hypothesis:** proposed mechanism that should be tested.
- **Validated locally:** our own reproducible findings, with dates, sample, controls and uncertainty.

Do not elevate a hypothesis into a fact because an agent repeated it. Avoid universal formulas for attention and algorithm performance. Audience behavior depends on platform, creator, story quality, intent and context.

### Suggested hypotheses
- An emotionally recognizable opening might improve three-second retention among a selected niche.
- A consistent recurring identity might increase follow rate, independent of raw view count.
- A satisfying reveal might increase sharing and the likelihood of future viewing.
- A clear expert explanation might increase saves more than likes.
- A relevant disclosed commercial recommendation might generate more qualified actions than an unrelated high-paying offer.

Each requires specific testing; none should be treated as guaranteed.

## Ethical boundaries
- No fabricated social proof, fake testimonials, disguised sponsored speech, falsified real-world interviews or invented eyewitness accounts.
- No misleading human identity, stolen voice, likeness, or impersonation. Make fiction and synthetic media clear as required.
- No intentional exploitation of grief, addiction, insecurity or vulnerable groups to maximize compulsive use.
- Do not infer sensitive individual attributes or construct covert personal-level psychographic targeting from private information.
- Prioritize useful, entertaining, accurately represented value and long-term trust.
- Review relevant platform rules and applicable advertising law before monetized distribution.

## What to measure

**Attention:** impressions where available, thumb-stop / initial retention / video starts (definitions are platform-specific).
**Engagement quality:** completion and watch-time distribution, saves, meaningful shares, sentiment, qualified comments.
**Relationship:** returning viewers, follow conversion, repeat viewing, audience feedback, trust signals where actually measured.
**Commercial:** product-market relevance, qualified intent, disclosed affiliate/partner conversions, repeat action, trust impact.
**Costs:** marginal production expense, time, rework, human review.
**Integrity:** corrections, misleading-content reports, compliance issues, audience dissatisfaction.

A psychology-driven creative strategy must be judged by both audience value and sustainable economics.
