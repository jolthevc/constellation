# Constellation: Audience, Format and Commerce Evidence Synthesis
**Research date:** 2026-10-28 | **Scope:** US-first, with clearly labeled global context | **Status:** sourced landscape evidence, not proof any proposed property will succeed.

## Executive findings

1. **Broadest demographic spine: YouTube.** Pew 2026 ever-use among US adults: 95% ages 18-29; 91% ages 30-49; 85% ages 50-64; 65% ages 65+. Instagram: 79/64/41/21%; TikTok: 65/45/31/11%. This is platform addressability, NOT reachable audience count or daily video demand. [SRC-011]
2. **A healthy network requires non-youth properties.** Pew 2026 shows Facebook usage at 78% among ages 30-49, 71% at 50-64 and 59% at 65+. AARP surveyed 3,838 people 50+ and reports ~90% social use, with privacy, ease of use and relevance as common friction. The two measures use different samples and survey wording. [SRC-011, SRC-016]
3. **Audio/conversation is not simply Gen Z.** Edison 2026: 58% of Americans ages 12+ consumed a podcast in the preceding month, 68% of those ages 35-54 did, and 57% of ages 12+ have both listened to and watched a podcast. One can test natural, clearly fictional conversational media without claiming a fake real interview. [SRC-012]
4. **Format selection depends on objective and platform.** Buffer's 2026 engagement report, based on Buffer-published content, found median Instagram engagement per reach around 6.90% for carousels vs 3.31% for Reels, while a distinct earlier reach sample favored Reels for discovery. TikTok video in Buffer's sample had a 3.39% median engagement rate versus 1.92% for carousel/photo. Neither result proves that swapping formats alone will cause lift. [SRC-013]
5. **Cross-platform fandom is a graph opportunity.** Deloitte 2026 reports 55% of its US *fan respondents* engage with the object of their fandom across multiple platforms, 71% among Gen Z fans. 44% of fans report discovering on social then consuming or purchasing elsewhere. This supports cross-format journeys, but does not measure Constellation audience duplication or purchases. [SRC-014]
6. **Original work is structural, not cosmetic.** Meta states 75% of US Instagram recommendation content was original in Q4 2025, up 10 percentage points. This is a platform report of recommendations, not an estimate of individual creator reach or an excuse to copy a popular account. [SRC-020]
7. **Strong audience monetization cannot be inferred from following.** IAB's creator ad estimate is $37B for 2025 and $44B projected for 2026; it also notes advertiser problems with creator selection and outcomes. This confirms an established buying channel, not demand for any specific new property. [SRC-015]
8. **Practical home content has visible decision context.** HIRI Q1 2026 reports 24% citing social media in their home-project research, versus 59% search engines; Houzz's own 2026 homeowner sample reported $20K median completed renovation spend for 2025. Houzz respondents are not nationally representative; commercial fit requires independently measured user interest. [SRC-017, SRC-023]
9. **Gaming and hobby content is cross-generational.** ESA 2026 reports 67% of Americans play video games more than one hour per week and 66% of adult players cite relaxation/passing time. This is gaming participation, not evidence that any particular gaming account can monetize followers. [SRC-018]
10. **Global social psychology is multi-purpose.** GWI data summarized by DataReportal 2026 mid-year: 49% of surveyed global 16+ social users report keeping in touch as a reason for using platforms, 39.3% filling spare time, 30.1% reading news. Respondents select multiple reasons. A property must earn a context-specific psychological job; there is no single universal hook. [SRC-021]
11. **Teen usage is relevant research but not the initial commercial target.** Pew 2026 research of 1,458 US 13-17 year olds highlights entertainment, social connection and interests, with about 60% of teen TikTok users reporting product reviews as a reason for use. Any minors-oriented strategy requires stricter policies and age-appropriate safeguards; prefer adult 18+ pilots first. [SRC-019, SRC-027]
12. **Current platform signals are a measurement guide, not a ranking formula.** YouTube says Shorts performance includes chose-to-view, average view duration, percentage viewed and satisfaction signals. It does not privilege a particular format or prescribe minimum posting cadence. TikTok's hook/body/close advice is explicitly within ad creative guidance, and first-second hooking is creator advice rather than a universal measured threshold. [SRC-025, SRC-005, SRC-026]

## Implications for the graph
- Build an **age-diverse portfolio**, not merely 18-24 TikTok characters. Use YouTube as a likely cross-demographic discovery backbone while allowing TikTok/Instagram/Facebook and audio-native surfaces to vary by audience.
- Diversify psychological jobs, not just demographics: **laughter/identity**, **explanation/curiosity**, **actionable utility**, **aesthetic aspiration**, **community/fandom**, and **long-form companionship**.
- Differentiate **discovery vehicles** (e.g. short video), **relationship vehicles** (e.g. series and conversation), and **reference vehicles** (e.g. carousels and tutorials). They may be combined, but different forms win on different measured axes.
- Commercial intent is a separate dimension. A highly monetizable category can be poor for a specific personality; do not force product pitches where they break the creative promise.
- Platform and cohort statistics tell us where to **look**, not what any individual viewer necessarily feels or believes. All psychographic hypotheses require independent creative tests.
- Observe overlap carefully. Multiple follows across network accounts do not equal deduplicated reach. Owned opt-in relationships may later reduce algorithm dependence.

## Evidence grading
**A:** survey or official platform data supports precisely defined *population/metric*; does not support a content-format causal interpretation. **B:** observational platform data supports a directional association within a provider's selected sample. **C:** editorial hypothesis about content niches or production economics, not yet first-party validated.

| Claim | Grade | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube adoption across adult ages | A | Pew national ever-use in defined age bands |
| Podcast consumption 35-54 | A | Edison audio OR video podcast monthly |
| Engagement of IG carousel vs Reel | B | Provider-specific nonrandom posts, outcomes depend on metric |
| Relatable format gets more follows than faceless format | C | Test rather than assume |
| First-frame outcome reveal improves completion in our account | C | Needs controlled variants and first-party analytics |
| Home-repair content is commercial | B/C | External homeowner spend exists; actual referral interest untested |
| One account increases unduplicated reach of the graph | C | Requires overlap methodology and cohort analytics |

## Limitations, gaps and next studies
1. No reliable study here measures **specific creator formats by detailed demographic** with both reach and conversion outcomes, production cost and organic follower loyalty. We cannot legitimately infer such rankings.
2. Buffer, Metricool and social-analytics vendors use selected customers and differing denominators. Save raw definition and cohort before comparisons.
3. Public views cannot reveal per-second retention, save rates, follower gender, purchase conversion or audience overlap. Access only through owner-authorized analytics.
4. National surveys generally describe platform adoption, not interest segment size, creative taste, purchase readiness, or synthetic-host acceptance.
5. Platform IP/AI disclosure rules change; verify before publication.
6. Need creator-level original case study sampling, including **median** and weak posts, with dated URLs and **no fabricated metrics**. See `format-and-creator-audit-plan.md`.

## Decision lens for initial research priorities
Use three divergent pilot hypotheses rather than prematurely pick a universal winner:
- **Broad entertainment + curiosity:** faceless original story/micro-doc or character comedy. Objective: discovery, sharing and follow conversion. Evidence from platform breadth and fandom; genre performance unproven.
- **Practical utility:** repair, home economics, craft or consumer guidance. Objective: qualified repeat view, saves and commercial relevance. Evidence from homeowner and format studies; property outcomes unproven.
- **Identity/community:** explicitly fictional conversational comedy, gaming culture, fandom or hobby. Objective: relationship and repeat viewing. Evidence from podcast/fandom/gaming studies; synthetic character viability unproven.

This research directs **what to investigate and test**; it is not a forecast of follower growth, CPM, CAC or content ROAS.
