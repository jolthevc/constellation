# The Attention, Retention, Payoff and Relationship Standard
Status: mandatory creative standard for every Constellation property and published content package
Research review: 2026-10-28
Owner: editorial; changes to a property-specific implementation require human approval during the pilot.

## The principle
Every piece of content must answer: (1) why stop, (2) why stay, (3) what is delivered, (4) why interact/share/save, and (5) why return to this property. **Earn** attention with compelling substance; fulfill the expectation created by the opening. A compelling piece can succeed without any spoken CTA.

Every property translates this into its own distinctive grammar. Humor, documentary, aspirational aesthetics, practical advice, animated editorial, fictional hosts and quiet art do not use the same opening or pacing conventions. The universal rule is a designed viewer experience, not uniform content.

## Five creative contracts
1. **Scroll-stop:** the first visible frame, opening sound/line and early reveal show the relevant audience an immediate reason to care. Use a specific tension, real surprise, recognizability, beautiful visual, question, demonstrable result, conflict or delight. Make first-frame clarity an editorial test, **not** a claimed universal algorithm cutoff.
2. **Stay:** promise an achievable reward, then move toward it. Show escalating evidence, progressive reveals, escalating comedy, new scenes, demonstrations, micro-payoffs or meaningful tension. No filler or mechanical jump cuts merely to reset attention.
3. **Payoff:** deliver what was promised in-video. Humor lands, story resolves, transformation is shown, answer is given, emotional truth resonates, utility is demonstrated. If content is intentionally episodic, each installment still needs standalone value.
4. **Interaction:** design a legitimate share/save/comment/replay/follow reason. Shares are usually earned by identity, social relevance, humor, usefulness or surprise; saves by future utility or reference value; comments by real questions or constructive debate. No mandatory CTA.
5. **Relationship:** every post strengthens the property's long-term reason to exist. An account with 10M views and no trustworthy reason to return has not necessarily created network value.

## Before an idea is approved
- Specify property ID, audience ID, context, primary viewer reward, mood, platform/surface and proposed length.
- State **exactly** what the viewer receives at the end.
- Write or storyboard at least three substantively distinct openings where appropriate; include first frame, 0-1s, 1-3s, 3-6s and the first real value delivered.
- Select intended primary behavioral outcome, secondary outcomes and trade-offs. Do not pretend every piece should maximize all metrics.
- Define opening promise, visible progression, payoff location, optional CTA and why the CTA feels natural in the property's voice.
- Predict failure modes: confusion, generic phrasing, repetition, synthetic awkwardness, broken promise, weak editing, false certainty, audience misfit.
- Human editor approves high-stakes claims, synthetic-media presentation, and publication during pilot.

## Examples of property-sensitive translation
- Dry comic: visually absurd cold open -> recognition/escalation -> real punchline -> optional comment by peers.
- Quiet interiors: arresting visual composition -> progressive reveal -> complete before/after -> share or save for inspiration. No forced narration or loud bait.
- Expert DIY: show actual failure or finished result -> explain steps with proof -> usable result/caveat -> save as reference.
- Fictional podcast: intriguing exchange -> real conversational progression -> amusing/insightful resolution -> recurring character chemistry. Disclose fictional/synthetic nature as required.
- Faceless investigation: concrete surprising fact -> documented question -> evidence and reversals -> carefully qualified conclusion -> share for insight.

## Hard creative and trust gates
- Hook must accurately represent the content; never fabricate evidence, lived experiences, testimonials, experts, spontaneous street encounters or product use.
- Synthetic scenes / people / voices require permissions and platform-appropriate disclosures. Label reconstructed scenes where needed.
- Avoid repetitive comment-word bait, deceptive cliffhangers, artificial loops, coercive engagement, manufactured outrage and empty promises.
- A “comment KEYWORD for resource” CTA is conditional: the resource must exist, delivery must be technically and policy-compliant, any necessary consent/privacy information must be clear, and the video must deliver worthwhile value **without** the comment.
- Each creator needs a recognizable set of **allowed** hooks, payoff styles, interaction behaviors, and “never do” anti-patterns.
- Originality, truthful representation and long-term audience trust override short-term engagement tricks.

## Measurement and continuous optimization
Interpret platform metrics according to platform definitions and available data. Diagnose (a) scroll-stop, (b) retention distribution, (c) ending satisfaction, (d) qualified interaction and (e) return/follow behavior. Compare within property, similar duration, topic and surface. Use planned experiments; do not infer causation from one viral post.
