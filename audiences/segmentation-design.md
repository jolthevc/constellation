# Segmentation and Coverage Design
Updated: 2026-10-28 | Status: planning ontology, not validated market data

## The principle
To achieve broad coverage, represent audiences using overlapping descriptive **contexts**, not one exhaustive list of people. A viewer can simultaneously be a recent graduate, runner, home cook, film fan and urban renter. The SEG and INT catalogs are not partitions of the population, and their entries should not be counted as independent markets.

## Four complementary axes
1. **Life context and circumstances**: college, first home, career transition, caring for family, moving, retirement planning. An index label does not imply a uniform psychology.
2. **Interest and subculture**: sport, gaming, comedy, travel, fashion, cooking, design, hobbies, work communities, local interests and dozens more.
3. **Media motivation**: escapism, laughter, curiosity, reassurance, utility, status and identity expression, companionship, aesthetic gratification and learning. Motivations depend on the individual and viewing moment.
4. **Content behavior**: passive short-video scroll, active search, save-for-later, shared content, long-form watch, audio-first consumption, group discussion and repeated series attendance.

Language and region may become relevant when justified by actual content and opportunity; age context and audience maturity matter for platform rules. Do not profile individuals by inferred sensitive traits.

## How to reason about a target
“People interested in skincare” is an interest, not a single demographic. A practical, scientifically grounded tutorial and a high-fashion visual presentation may serve different psychological jobs within that interest. The property should choose an original promise and select compatible content formats/hook/payoff, rather than targeting a simplistic stereotype.

## Hypothesis -> evidence
All discovery rows in `segments/index.csv` and `interests/index.csv` start `candidate/hypothesis`. Research creates a dated specific profile. Content tests generate platform-specific observed evidence. Only legitimate aggregate data may establish actual property reach in `../graph/coverage.csv`.

## Commercial adjacency
`commercial-interest-map.csv` enumerates ideas for what a property covering an interest might credibly discuss or recommend. This does not show willingness to pay, actual demand, ability to reach particular individuals, a validated CAC, or the right to promote regulated products.

## Portfolio questions
- Which combination of context + interest + psychological job is underrepresented?
- Do we have a credible new creative approach, not a derivative clone?
- What would add *incremental* audience reach or repeated legitimate touchpoints?
- Can it deliver satisfying content consistently and cheaply enough at a high bar?
- What do we actually know, and what must be tested before launch?
