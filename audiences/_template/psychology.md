## Definition and diversity
Who is in this audience? Why is the segment useful? Distinguish age, income, geography and demographics from psychographic behavior. Identify meaningful subgroups, nonrepresentative sampling and uncertainties.

## Context and media occasions
Platforms and situations of use, attention budget, routines, social settings, device context and discovery sources.

## Core psychological jobs
Rank actual evidence for: entertainment, escape, belonging, learning, curiosity, identity, aspiration, reassurance, humor, mastery, sensory pleasure, status, agency, and practical utility. Record unsupported items as hypotheses.

## Self-concept and social identity
Values, aspirations, self-expression, reference groups, community norms, humor, cultural language, admired figures, disliked cliches. Avoid treating averages as individual facts.

## Emotional tension and rewards
What do they want to feel? What questions are they trying to answer? What constitutes a satisfying piece of content? Note potential vulnerabilities without proposing to exploit them.
