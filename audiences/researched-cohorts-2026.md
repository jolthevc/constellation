Source-backed macro data, then clearly separated hypotheses | 2026-10-28

## Platform access by age (US adults who *ever use* site)
Derived directly from [Pew 2026](../research/sources.csv), SRC-011. This is an accessible population proportion, **not** view share or monetizable reach.

| Age | YouTube | Instagram | TikTok | Facebook | Reddit |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 18-29 | 95% | 79% | 65% | 69% | 40% |
| 30-49 | 91% | 64% | 45% | 78% | 35% |
| 50-64 | 85% | 41% | 31% | 71% | 18% |
| 65+ | 65% | 21% | 11% | 59% | 6% |

Pew surveyed 5,511 US adults January to June 2026. Overall margin ±1.8 percentage points; subgroup errors greater. Differences between age bands do not reveal content preference or user exclusivity. Full dataset in `../research/datasets/platform-adoption-us-2026.csv`.

## Broad directional coverage bets
**18-29:** multiple meaningful platforms; potential personality/comedy/trends and fandom; do not assume age uniformity. Existing research profiles for college students and early-career segments use 18-29 as a proxy **only**, not a campus-specific sample.

**30-49:** YouTube plus Facebook and Instagram are widespread; video/audio podcast listening especially high among 35-54 in Edison (68% monthly, includes podcast watching). Potential utility, identity and conversation formats, subject to real tests.

**50-64 and 65+:** YouTube/Facebook remain significant; AARP reports high 50+ social and streaming use, with privacy and usability concerns. Do not portray older people as technology-averse or universally seeking retirement advice. Segment by hobby, life context and psychological job.

**Teens 13-17:** Pew 2025-26 finds high platform usage and entertainment motivation; current Constellation pilots should favor adults to avoid minors-related commercial/profiling challenges.

## Audience isn't demographic alone
Intersection examples, all **hypotheses**: 65-year-old gamer may want playful challenge; 23-year-old first-time homeowner may want reliable repair guidance; 42-year-old film fan may want long-form documentaries; 28-year-old finance worker may be seeking comedy relief. Choose psychological job by topic and actual research, not age alone.

## Survey interpretation matrix
- Pew: national cross-sectional platform access, not reach overlap.
- AARP: 50+ tech use and hesitations, different age frame/questions.
- Edison: podcast audio OR video and online-audio adoption, not synthetic-host preference.
- Deloitte: fandom participation and cross-platform journeys, fan subgroup.
- Buffer/Metricool: post outcomes within provider-selected accounts, not audience-by-age conversion.
- HIRI/Houzz: home project research and spending, not owner revenue or clickthroughs.
